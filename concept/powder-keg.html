Game concept · working title

Powder Keg

A pausable grand strategy game where every nation is a countryball, the clock starts in 1914 or 1936, and the history books stop being right the moment he makes his first choice.

What I think

It is a strong idea and it fits what we already built. Fort and Frontier's parchment map, telegram-style event cards and pausable clock carry straight over. Three things I would change from the game he knows, so it stays fun and can actually be finished:

  • Fewer buttons, bigger consequences. That game has hundreds of menus. This one has five panels, and almost every click changes how another country feels about him.
  • Fronts, not units. He assigns armies to a front and picks a stance. He never has to drag forty divisions around.
  • The story is the point. The Chronicle newspaper writes up his timeline as he plays, and the end screen sets it beside what really happened. He learns the real history by breaking it.

How a session plays

  1. The clock runsDays tick by at three speeds. He can pause at any time, and the game pauses itself whenever a telegram arrives.
  2. He steersPick the next Plan, split factories between building and arming, send diplomats, move armies to fronts.
  3. Telegrams arriveStory moments with two or three choices each. Some are history knocking. Some are Fate cards: harvests, strikes, scandals, storms.
  4. The world answersEvery other nation is run by the computer with its own personality. They remember what he did and change sides because of it.

Why no two runs match

The game keeps a ledger for the whole world: how every nation feels about every other nation, who has promised what to whom, and a list of facts such as “Belgium was invaded” or “the conference in London succeeded”. A telegram can only appear when its conditions in the ledger are met, so each choice opens some futures and closes others.

Each computer nation has three dials: ambition, caution and how long it holds a grudge. Britain is cautious and never forgets. Italy is ambitious and joins whoever looks like winning. The same event lands differently depending on where those dials and the ledger stand.

Fate cards come from a shuffled deck with a numbered seed. A new game shuffles a new deck, so luck differs each time. If he wants to test a what-if properly he can replay the same seed, and then the only thing that changes history is him.

The cast

Nations are countryballs in the usual style: wobbly hand-drawn outlines, white eyes with no pupils, no arms or legs, and feelings shown with the eyes alone. On the map a ball's face is his diplomacy screen. Angry eyes mean that country is close to turning on him. The well-known in-jokes are kept: Britain's top hat and monocle, the German Empire as a tall rectangle, and Poland with its flag upside down.

They talk, too. Short speech bubbles in each ball's own voice react to what he just did.

Two starting years

1914

The Great War, or not

  • What if the powers talk instead of mobilising?
  • What if Germany leaves Belgium alone and Britain stays home?
  • What if Italy keeps its promise to its old allies?
  • What if America never crosses the ocean?
  • What if Poland gets back on the map early?
1936

The second round

  • Starts from real 1936, or from the world his own 1914 game ended in.
  • What if Britain and France stand firm early?
  • What if the League of Nations has teeth?
  • What if a gentler treaty in 1919 means there is no second war to fight?

A note on tone. The second war includes regimes that did terrible things. The game treats that as history to be told plainly in the Chronicle, never as a mechanic or a joke. It uses period flags without hate symbols, and the dictators are not playable heroes.

How I would build it

  1. A playable 1914. The map of Europe, the clock, five playable powers, telegrams, diplomacy, the Chronicle and about sixty story and Fate cards. Wars resolve on their own at this stage. This is already a game he can play and replay.
  2. The war. Armies and fronts, factories, research, generals, and the Treaty table when the fighting stops.
  3. 1936. The second starting year, including carrying a finished 1914 world forward.
  4. Polish. Save slots, sound, the “your timeline against real history” end screen, and a card he can share with friends.

It would be built the same way as Fort and Frontier: one page that runs in the browser on a computer or tablet, with saves.

Three things to ask him

  1. Which country does he want to play first? I will build that one's Plans tree and voice with the most care.
  2. How old is he, and how long does he like to play in one sitting? That sets the reading level of the telegrams and the length of a run.
  3. Does he want to keep the name? Powder Keg is only a placeholder.
